# Career Assessment and Decision Support

OrganicAI Compass treats career assessment as self-reflection and professional exploration. Personality, work-style, interests, values, skills, AI readiness, and change-readiness signals can help a user ask better questions, but they do not prove that a person is suited or unsuited for a role.

## Limitations

Assessment results are based on self-reported answers and prototype scoring rules. They should not be used as psychological diagnosis, hiring criteria, clinical evidence, or a guarantee of career success. Users should confirm, correct, or reject interpretations that do not fit their lived context.

## Career Interests

Career-interest dimensions describe preferred activities and environments. They should not be translated directly into one profession. A user with Artistic, Investigative, and Social interests might explore design research, learning experience design, AI product design, or communication-heavy technology roles, but each option still requires evidence, practice, and market validation.

## Work Values

Work values can create useful tensions. A role may fit creativity and autonomy while conflicting with stability, predictable structure, or work-life balance. Decision support should make these conflicts visible so the user can decide what matters most.

## Transferable Skills

Transferable skills connect previous experience to adjacent roles. Client communication may support UX research or consulting. Project coordination may support technical project management or AI product operations. Visual design may support AI product design. Teaching may support learning experience design. Architectural systems thinking may support spatial computing, digital twins, or complex product mapping.

Self-reported skills should not be treated as objectively verified. Evidence status matters: supported by experience, supported by project, supported by certification, or practically verified.

## AI Literacy and Readiness

AI literacy includes conceptual understanding, critical evaluation, hallucination awareness, source evaluation, privacy, and responsible automation. AI readiness includes practical tool use, workflow integration, technical confidence, available infrastructure, and willingness to learn.

These levels describe current readiness, not personal worth. Readiness can change through practice and support.

## Professional Transition

A career transition should be tested before it becomes a commitment. Useful experiments include reviewing job descriptions, interviewing a professional, completing a short course, building a small project, shadowing a role, or testing an AI-assisted workflow in the current profession.

The safest first recommendation is often to augment the current profession before pursuing full reskilling. A staged roadmap can then validate the direction, close skill gaps, demonstrate capability, and only later support market transition.
